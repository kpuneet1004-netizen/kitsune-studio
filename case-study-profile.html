The challenge
in plain language.

A resume communicates a timeline, but it rarely gives a complete sense of someone’s working style, selected projects, or interests. A personal website can connect those pieces into one useful introduction.

This concept focuses on scannability and clarity. It is not a real person’s profile and does not invent employment history, qualifications, endorsements, or outcomes.

The design
approach.

Separate identity from detail. A quiet sidebar provides a clear identity anchor, while the main content carries the introduction.

Make the information skimmable. Short sections and explicit labels help visitors find experience, selected work, and contact details quickly.

Design for the next action. Contact information and portfolio links should be easy to reach without forcing visitors to read everything first.

01 / CONTENT

02 / USABILITY

Easy to scan

Clear headings and deliberate spacing help hiring managers and collaborators find key details.

03 / RESPONSIVE

Simple on mobile

The two-column concept becomes a single readable flow on narrow screens.

This is a self-initiated design concept using placeholder identity and copy. It does not represent a real individual or claim hiring, conversion, or career outcomes.
