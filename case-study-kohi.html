01 / THE BRAND

Build a digital space that feels like the café itself.

Kōhī is a fictional café brand concept for this design study, not a verified operating business. Its identity imagines a calm, Japanese-inspired kissaten: considered coffee, a small menu of comforting food, and an unhurried neighbourhood atmosphere.

BRAND PERSONALITY

Warm, thoughtful, unhurried

Editorial rather than trendy. Human rather than corporate. Quiet confidence without visual clutter.

INTENDED AUDIENCE

People looking for a pause

Local coffee drinkers, casual meet-ups, remote workers, and guests exploring a café before visiting.

CORE PROMISE

A little room to slow down

Use the digital experience to communicate the mood, show what is served, and make the visit easy to plan.

02 / THE BRIEF

Turn atmosphere into a clear next step.

The supplied project files describe a front-end café website concept. A formal client brief and user-research findings were not included, so these requirements are presented as design goals inferred from the delivered experience—not as client-validated requirements.

Design goals

  • Communicate the café’s personality in the first screen.
  • Make story, menu, location, and visit information easy to find.
  • Show food and drinks with clear imagery and labels.
  • Support comfortable browsing on phones as well as desktop.
  • Offer a reservation-request interaction without implying a real booking is made.

Experience requirements

  • Responsive navigation with clear section anchors.
  • Editorial story and café-philosophy sections.
  • Photo gallery and featured menu / specials.
  • Visit details and a front-end reservation form.
  • Accessible labels, keyboard focus, meaningful alt text, and reduced-motion support.
03 / THE PROBLEM

A café site must do more than look delicious.

Hospitality websites can lean too heavily on atmosphere and leave practical questions unanswered—or become functional directories with no sense of place. Kōhī explores a balance between emotional appeal and useful information.

CHALLENGE 01

Communicate the feeling quickly

Visitors should recognise the café’s character before they scroll through a long story.

CHALLENGE 02

Make the menu easy to scan

Food photography, names, and categories should work together rather than compete for attention.

CHALLENGE 03

Remove friction from planning

Navigation and visit information should be easy to reach, especially on a small screen.

04 / THE SOLUTION

One warm, editorial journey from discovery to visit.

Information architecture

Hero → café story → philosophy → gallery → featured specials → wider menu → visit / reservation request → footer.

The order introduces the mood first, then provides more detail for visitors who are deciding what to order or whether to visit.

Interaction design

  • Mobile menu with Escape and outside-click behaviour.
  • Scroll-reveal effects with reduced-motion preferences respected.
  • Continuous menu marquee with pause / resume control.
  • Form validation and a demo confirmation state.
  • Clear focus styles and semantic section structure.

06 / MOBILE-FIRST UX

Keep the essentials within easy reach.

Small-screen priorities

  • Navigation collapses into a touch-friendly menu.
  • Content becomes a single-column reading flow.
  • Menu cards and gallery images reflow without horizontal page scrolling.
  • Form fields use full-width targets and clear labels.
  • Typography scales with viewport size and content remains readable.

Accessibility & resilience

  • Semantic landmarks and descriptive image text.
  • Keyboard-visible focus states.
  • Reduced-motion preference support.
  • Lazy-loaded below-the-fold photography.
  • Form validation and a visible demo response.

08 / HANDOFF & LIMITATIONS

A clear concept, with honest boundaries.

Included in the concept

  • Responsive front-end website and visual system.
  • Story, philosophy, gallery, featured specials, menu, and visit sections.
  • Interactive navigation, motion, and reservation-request UI.
  • Basic semantic and accessibility-minded implementation.

Before a real launch

  • Replace placeholder address, hours, contact details, menu items, and provisional prices with approved information.
  • Connect the reservation flow to a real booking system and implement privacy / server-side validation.
  • Confirm photography rights and test external image loading.
  • Run real-device browser checks and a performance audit.

Project integrity note: this is a self-initiated concept based on the supplied files. No commissioned client work, user research, conversion lift, performance score, or business outcome is claimed. The design goals above are inferred from the implementation and project notes.
